Septic System Questions, Answered Straight

The questions we hear most, from cost to permits to how long a system actually lasts.

How much does a septic system cost in BC?

It depends heavily on system type, soil conditions, and site access — a system with pumps and controls on tougher ground costs more than a straightforward gravity field on easy ground. There's no single number that means much without a site visit, but we'll always give you a firm quote before any work starts, not just a ballpark.

How long does a septic system last?

A well-designed, properly maintained conventional system commonly lasts 20 to 30+ years. Pressure distribution systems with mechanical components (pumps, control panels) depend more on maintenance — pumps and floats are wear items that get serviced or replaced well before the system itself needs replacing. Soil conditions, usage patterns, and regular tank pump-outs all affect the real-world number.

What are the signs my septic system is failing?

Slow drains throughout the house, sewage odour near the tank or field, standing water or unusually lush grass over the field, gurgling drains, or an alarm sounding on a pump system. Sewage backing up into the house is the one sign that means call immediately rather than wait and watch.

Do I need a permit for septic work in BC?

Yes — every septic system in BC requires it. Any new installation, replacement, or repair that touches the design or components of the system must be designed, filed, and constructed by an Authorized Person (a Registered Onsite Wastewater Practitioner or professional engineer) under the Sewerage System Regulation, with documentation submitted to your regional health authority (Interior Health or Northern Health, depending on location). There's no exception for smaller or simpler systems — all of them go through the same filing process.

How often should I pump my septic tank?

A common guideline is every 3 to 5 years for an average household, but actual frequency depends on tank size, household size, and water usage. Systems with pumps or mechanical components benefit from checking those components at the same time the tank gets pumped.

Can I install my own septic system in BC?

No — not on your own. Under BC's Sewerage System Regulation, only an Authorized Person (a Registered Onsite Wastewater Practitioner or professional engineer) can design and file a sewerage system. A homeowner can do some of the physical labour on certain system types, but only under the direct supervision of that Authorized Person, who still designs the system, still files it with your health authority, and is still accountable for it. There's no path to a fully owner-designed or owner-installed system in BC.

What happens after my septic system is installed?

A completed installation includes commissioning (confirming dose volumes, alarms, and pump cycling match the design) and a documentation package — as-built drawings, a commissioning report, and an owner's information sheet. Within 30 days of completion, the Authorized Person files a Letter of Certification (LoC) with your health authority, confirming the system was built to the approved, filed design.
