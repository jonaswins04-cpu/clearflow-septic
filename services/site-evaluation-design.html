Site Evaluation & System Design

The goal of a site evaluation is simple: find out what your soil, water table, and lot will actually support, and design the system that fits. Not to sell you a particular kind of system.

Call 778-392-6313 Request a Site Visit

What a site evaluation actually decides

Every property is different, and the ground itself makes the call — not the other way around. A site evaluation exists to find out what a specific property can support: whether that's a straightforward conventional field trenched into native soil, or whether the soil, water table, or bedrock rule that out and the system needs to be built above grade instead. Both are legitimate, code-compliant outcomes. The testing tells us which one your property needs.

What we test

  • Test pits — dug at proposed system locations to expose the actual soil profile: texture, structure, depth to water table, and depth to bedrock or any other restrictive layer
  • Percolation (perc) testing — measures how quickly the soil absorbs water, which determines how much absorption area a system needs to handle your household's daily flow
  • Slope and usable area — affects layout options and whether setback requirements from wells, property lines, and water bodies can be met

What the results determine

Conventional trenched field

If the soil percs adequately, the water table sits deep enough, and there's no shallow bedrock or restrictive layer, a conventional gravity or pressure distribution field trenched into native soil is usually the simplest and least expensive option — no pumps or mechanical components required.

Above-ground system

If the soil won't perc well enough, the water table is too high, or bedrock sits close to the surface, a conventional field isn't an option. The system gets built up instead of dug in — see our sand mound design page for how that works.

Usage matters too — household size, number of bedrooms, and daily water use all factor into sizing, regardless of which system type the soil points to.

What you get

Complete drawings, design rationale, and specifications, filed with your regional health authority (Interior Health or Northern Health, depending on where the property sits) before construction begins, as required under BC's Sewerage System Regulation.
