Sand Mound & Pressure Distribution Design

When the soil on a property won't perc, the water table sits too high, or bedrock is close to surface, a conventional gravity field isn't an option. A sand mound gives that property a fully code-compliant system — built up, not dug in.

What a sand mound is, and why it's used

A sand mound is an engineered, above-grade septic field built from specified mound sand, placed over the natural ground surface rather than trenched into it. It's the standard solution in BC under the Standard Practice Manual (SPM V3) when a site can't support a conventional in-ground field — most commonly because of:

  • Poor or very slow percolation (clay-heavy or compacted soils)
  • A high seasonal water table
  • Shallow bedrock or a shallow restrictive layer
  • Limited usable area that still needs to meet setback requirements

The mound sand itself becomes the treatment media. Effluent is dosed into the mound under pressure, distributed evenly across the bed through a network of laterals, and treated as it passes down through the engineered sand before reaching native soil.

How the design process works

1. Site evaluation

We start with test pits and percolation testing to understand what's actually happening underground — soil texture and structure at depth, depth to water table, depth to any restrictive layer, and slope. This is what determines whether a site needs a mound at all, or whether a conventional field is still possible.

2. Design & sizing

Mound size is driven by daily design flow (based on bedroom count or fixture count), the soil's application rate, and the site's usable area and slope. We design the absorption area, the pressure distribution network, dosing volume, and pump or siphon sizing to match — every mound is sized to the specific property, not a standard template.

3. Filing & approval

Completed drawings and design rationale are filed with your regional health authority (Interior Health or Northern Health, depending on where the property sits) before construction begins, as required under BC's Sewerage System Regulation.

4. Construction

Because we design and build, the mound that gets built is the one that was engineered — no gap between the drawing and what ends up in the ground. Construction follows SPM V3 specifications for sand quality, compaction, and layering.

What it costs, roughly

Sand mounds cost more than a conventional field — more material, more equipment time, and pump/control equipment are usually part of the system. Every site is different enough in soil, access, and size that a real number only comes from a site visit; we don't quote mound work sight unseen.

All sand mound design work is supervised by a Registered Onsite Wastewater Practitioner (ROWP) under BC's Sewerage System Regulation, with filings submitted to Interior Health or Northern Health.
