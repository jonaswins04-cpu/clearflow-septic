Septic Systems — Invermere & Columbia Valley

From Invermere and Radium north to Golden, the Columbia Valley is one of our two home-base regions — and its lakes and wetlands mean a lot of that work is sand mound design.

A different set of site conditions

The Columbia Valley sits along the Columbia River wetlands, with Windermere Lake and the surrounding lakefront and near-lake properties bringing a challenge that's less common further south: a high seasonal water table. Combined with a lot of recreational and seasonal-use properties, that pushes a higher share of Columbia Valley jobs toward engineered sand mounds rather than conventional fields.

What that means for a build here

  • Sites near the lake or wetland areas often need a mound regardless of soil quality, simply because of water table depth
  • Seasonal-use properties (cabins, recreational lots) sometimes have different design flow considerations than a full-time residence — worth flagging at the site visit
  • Setback requirements from the lake, wetlands, and neighbouring wells tend to be a bigger factor in site layout here than in drier inland areas

Golden & Radium

Golden sits at the northern edge of our regular service area — different valley, different soil profile again, generally more workable ground than right along the lakeshore, but still worth a proper test pit before assuming anything. Radium and the properties around the hot springs corridor see similar considerations to Invermere given the shared valley geography.

Real estate work in the valley

A meaningful share of Columbia Valley properties are recreational or investment purchases, which means septic assessments tied to a sale come up often here — buyers wanting to know what they're inheriting on a lake property before subject removal.

All design and installation work is supervised by a Registered Onsite Wastewater Practitioner (ROWP) under BC's Sewerage System Regulation, filed with Interior Health.
